Rasmitra category screen

On our size-and-growth metric, spices ranks first and premixes fourth of the four categories that pass the Board's constraints. Together they reach 18.5% of FY24 revenue in the base case and 27.5% in the stretch case, against a target of 30%.

How the screen works

The Board's constraints decide which categories may enter. Market size and growth then set the order.

RTC mixes is bigger and growing faster, so it beats premixes on both criteria our metric puts first. Premixes wins every Board-fit row. Keeping it means arguing fit over attractiveness, which is Tension 03 in the case.

Our case for premixes: it is the only category that peaks in winter, it can run in a plant that sits at 34% utilisation in Oct-Dec, and it repeats the pattern of the premium variant that worked: existing plant, pack and route.

How the order moves with the size-growth split

Distributor and budget weights stay at our metric's. Size and growth share the rest of the points.

Spices leads once market size gets 35 points or more. Premixes reaches the top two only when size gets 20 points or fewer, and there spices falls to fourth. No split puts spices and premixes in the top two together.

Board constraint check

Distributor fit averages the three tests on a 1-5 scale. Budget fit averages contract-manufacturing availability and in-house capex. A category needs both to pass.

Scenarios

Base. Launches on plan: premixes for the first winter, spices one quarter later, a third category a year after.

Stretch. Same launch dates; half as much share again, and reach grows as we add outlets in Regions 3-4 and quick-commerce.

Downside. Every launch slips a season and shares come in lower.

Our reach is the share of India's category sales that passes through the outlets and accounts we already serve. A ramp is the share of the year-three level reached in that year.
